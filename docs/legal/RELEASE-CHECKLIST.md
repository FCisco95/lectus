# Lectus release and legal review checklist

Status: **internal preparation, not release approval or a compliance certificate**. Reviewed 2026-10-21 against the working tree described in [IMPLEMENTATION-FACTS.md](IMPLEMENTATION-FACTS.md). Entries are open until supported by named evidence. A privacy notice, installer checkbox or license inventory does not establish compliance by itself.

## 1. Publisher and market decisions — publisher supplies facts

- [ ] Confirm the full legal name of the publisher (an individual is possible), establishment/country, service/postal contact where required, support address and privacy-rights contact. Do not substitute a brand, GitHub username or invented company.
- [ ] Confirm supported countries/markets, consumer vs business distribution, intended age range and any excluded regulated uses. Country-specific requirements need local review; do not infer them from the developer's location.
- [ ] Describe the actual commercial arrangement: free access, token-holding membership condition, any payment/subscription/fees, acquisition links, support entitlement and update/support period. Assess consumer-law applicability to the actual arrangement, rather than assuming token gating means no consumer contract.
- [ ] Establish rights to distribute first-party code, product name, branding/artwork and all included dependencies/models. Decide the application's license separately from third-party licenses and publish accurate authorship/publisher metadata.
- [ ] Record the reviewer/counsel, final document versions, effective date, language versions and final source/artifact hashes. Draft placeholders remain explicitly marked and block claims that final legal terms are ready.

## 2. Privacy and consent — applicability/legal basis needs review

- [ ] Map roles and purposes per flow: local processing, cloud speech, cleanup/translation, CLI processing, wallet checks, downloads/updates, remote delivery, local API and support. Determine which operations make the publisher a controller/processor, which are user-controlled third-party services, and which laws apply. Local-only processing is a fact to assess, not a blanket exemption.
- [ ] If GDPR applies, provide relevant identity/contact, purpose/legal basis, recipients, transfers/safeguards, retention, rights/complaint information and other required disclosures at the appropriate collection point. The [European Commission's information duties](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/obligations_en) are the starting checklist; fill in actual facts rather than generic guarantees.
- [ ] Separate contractual acceptance from any processing consent. Choose and document a lawful basis for each relevant operation; where relying on consent, it must be specific, informed, freely given, affirmative and withdrawable. Do not preselect optional cloud processing or treat “I accept Terms” as consent to every data flow. [EDPB lawful-processing guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/process-personal-data-lawfully_en) and [EDPB consent FAQ](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/find-practical-info/faq_en?page=1) describe this distinction.
- [ ] Reconcile microphone notice with the actual 500 ms pre-roll and toggle behavior. Verify no capture occurs before the newly required gate, including startup/autostart, device change and restored settings. Clearly explain that cloud use can send pre-roll audio, that history holds final text, and that clipboard/recipient applications have separate retention.
- [ ] Review applicable special-category/bystander audio, children, access/erasure/export, retention/security, processor agreements, international transfers and incident-response obligations for the intended use. Assess DPIA/DPO/representative requirements rather than asserting they are universally required. Do not claim provider zero retention, training exclusion or hosting region without the chosen provider/account's current authoritative terms and configuration evidence.

## 3. Contract and mandatory consumer rights — counsel reviews applicability

- [ ] Explain concrete functionality, local-model/resource requirements, known speech/translation fallibility, membership gate and third-party dependencies. Avoid guaranteed accuracy, uninterrupted access or promises that every feature works offline.
- [ ] Preserve any mandatory consumer remedies and guarantees. Review the [EU digital-content rules](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/business-and-industry/contract-rules/digital-contracts/digital-contract-rules_en) and [Directive 2019/770 summary](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=legissum:4400471), including scope, exceptions, updates and national implementation. Broad “as is,” “no refunds” or liability wording must not purport to remove non-waivable rights.
- [ ] Determine whether withdrawal rights apply to the actual supply/payment flow and whether any digital-content exception is available. If relying on an exception, implement its specific consent/acknowledgement/confirmation conditions; an ordinary EULA acceptance is not evidence of them. Consult the [Consumer Rights Directive consolidated text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02011L0083-20260927) and applicable national law, including current online withdrawal-function requirements where relevant.
- [ ] Check cancellation, termination, feature/update changes, support, governing-law/jurisdiction and dispute terms against the confirmed countries and consumer/business status. Do not invent a governing country or assume a choice-of-law clause overrides mandatory consumer protections.
- [ ] Review token-related marketing and eligibility wording separately. The code checks a wallet's holdings; that does not establish regulatory treatment, asset rights, endorsement, return expectations or consumer-law exemption.

## 4. Third-party notices and model terms — release engineer plus legal reviewer

- [ ] Run `node scripts/collect-third-party-notices.mjs` on the exact locked source tree; inspect `legal/third-party/inventory.json` and `missing-notices.json`. Treat absent optional packages and source-only license headers explicitly. Reconcile Windows/macOS artifact contents with the conservative all-target/build/dev inventory before claiming completeness.
- [ ] Ship the required exact license/copyright/NOTICE texts for included Rust/npm code, native C/C++ vendor components and runtime redistributables. Preserve AND vs OR SPDX expressions. For any shipped MPL-covered code, establish the corresponding-source distribution mechanism and required notices; build/proc-macro presence alone does not prove shipment.
- [ ] Record exact model revision, source URL and artifact digest. Include Whisper/OpenAI, whisper.cpp/conversion and Silero notices as applicable; Qwen3-4B has Apache-2.0 terms, which must be matched to the exact selected GGUF. Do not generalize licenses across similarly named model families.
- [ ] Review the optional Gemma 3 download path under the [Gemma terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/terms). Provide the agreement, required Notice, incorporated use restrictions and modification notices as applicable. Do not describe Gemma 3 as Apache, assume a third-party host removes obligations, or treat a copied Notice alone as completion of every distribution condition.
- [ ] Bundle legal resources in the final installer and expose notices after installation, offline. Confirm package versions, native DLL/backend licenses, WebView2 and SDK redistributable obligations, and artwork provenance. License snapshots and metadata are evidence inputs, not a legal-compliance certificate.

## 5. Installer and final UX evidence — release engineer

- [ ] Verify installer metadata uses the confirmed publisher and real version/product description; test fresh install, upgrade, repair/uninstall behavior and persistence/removal of configuration, keys, tokens, history and downloaded models. Use isolated fixtures; do not erase the user's real data to test removal.
- [ ] Verify actual platform trust separately: Windows Authenticode certificate/publisher/timestamp, and macOS Developer ID/notarization where chosen. Tauri updater signatures are a distinct mechanism and do not prove either platform identity. Document any unsigned distribution clearly.
- [ ] Test legal/onboarding gates and optional cloud acknowledgement through both UI and backend commands. Decline/cancel must have a clear effect. Confirm old configs, hidden/autostart, restored acceptance, version changes and provider/model changes cannot bypass the intended requirement; capture no real speech or credentials in tests.
- [ ] Validate help, translations and errors against actual capabilities: recognition language vs output language, local vs cloud, CLI data flow, remote text vs audio, local API restrictions, history deletion limits and precise microphone behavior. Test keyboard access, readable legal text, links, focus, small windows and light/dark themes.
- [ ] Preserve the final receipts: source SHA plus dirty-tree diff hash or clean commit, test scope, model/artifact digests, installer/notice contents, signature checks, and reviewer decisions. Distinguish prepared, verified, signed, published, installed and accepted. Nothing in this checklist authorizes installation, publication, account changes or contacting third parties.
